Notes: Method and assumptions. Baseline: I rebuilt v2's size table by block and arm and got 930 sessions (probes 40; 52 full-execution; 790 capture; 48 on Fable). At v2's unit figures (capture $0.09 to $0.13; Fable 2.5x; full-execution $0.87 to $1.15 from v2's $45 to $60 for 52; probes $5 to $10) the list cost is $132 to $188, midpoint $158. That is 2.2 to 3.1 batch-1 multiples (batch 1 = $61.03), slightly under v2's printed $140 to $200 and 2.3 to 3.3x. Dollars throughout are list price and batch-1 multiples; I give no plan-usage percentage because none can be known. Dollar ranges set every capture session at its low or high figure together, so they are bounds rather than a probability interval.

Findings that change the size table. (1) Expected T is about 9, not 12. From the author's own P(R agrees) predictions and the admit-at-4-of-8 rule, I get an expected 9.0 admitted of 19 (D-set alone 5.2; replacements add 3.9); P(T at least 12) is 8% and P(T at least 10) is 39%. At T=9 the baseline is about 830 sessions, $118 to $169. The decision table needs T at least 12 for any 'no material increment' row, so those rows are probably unreachable as designed. (2) The table omits cells for screen-derived sets: the 5-6/8 sensitivity set (expected 7.2 tasks) and screen-derived anchors (expected 2.8). If run, they add about 86 and 73 sessions; I recommend not running them and dropping the 'sensitivity set added' item. (3) Anchors at 6 runs do not buy the registered pooled +/-0.10 check. In my simulation it passes 0 to 1.4% of the time unless both arms agree on every run; 6 runs buy specificity power only. (4) The efficient lever for E1 is runs on R and RS: 2 more runs per cell cost 4 sessions per task, and in my simulation lift power at T=9 from 0.71 to 0.86. A new task costs 28 sessions for about 0.03 to 0.05. That is why Plans A and B put R and RS at 8.

Caveats on my numbers. Power, TOST and specificity figures come from my own simulation (pure Python, independent runs, per-task R rate uniform 0.1 to 0.5, exact stratified permutation test, Wald interval with (x+0.5)/(n+1)), not v2's registered script; use them for orientation. Admission yields use the author's predictions, which round 2 raised and which are untested until the screen runs. Skill-loaded arms (RS, RL, D, S: 334 of the 790 capture sessions) probably cost more than a plain capture, since they add a Skill call and the document read; if they run 1.4x, add $12 to $17 to the baseline; probe I7 should measure it. The $0.97 full-execution figure may understate smoke and gate sessions on opus or fable workers (attempt 1: $1.4 to $5.8 each).
