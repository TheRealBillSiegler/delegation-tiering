# Outside reader

Summary: The trial asks which part of the owner's three-part delegation setup moves a Claude Code orchestrator's choice of subagent model toward the skill's tier ladder. The three parts are an always-loaded rule with a CLAUDE.md row, a skill the model invokes, and a PreToolUse gate that denies Agent calls with no model. Each session is a headless `claude -p` Opus 5.5 child with its own configuration directory, run on a frozen neutral Node fixture. Sessions are randomized across eight arms (no text, placebo, row alone, rule, rule plus the skill's rules inlined, rule plus skill, rule plus skill plus gate, skill alone), and an observer hook records and denies every delegation in the first delegating turn, then stops the session. Candidate tasks are screened in the rule-only arm (eight runs each) and enter the primary set only when at most four of eight runs agree with the author's label, which three Sonnet labelers reading the skill must match unanimously. The primary endpoint E1 (rule plus skill minus rule, label agreement, six runs per cell, exact stratified permutation test) is split by the inlined-rules arm into F1 and F2, checked for delivery, exposure and planted effects (M1 to M3), and fed to a decision table (keep the skill, inline its rules, keep the rule alone, or inconclusive); the gate is only described, in a small full-execution subset, and changes no branch.

Verdict: Not registrable as v2 stands. Three findings block registration. First, at the expected number of admitted tasks the decision branches are almost unreachable. Second, the decision table cannot be applied mechanically: signs are missing, rows overlap, and the M2 and specificity criteria are undefined, so a negative F2 can map to 'keep the skill'. Third, the owner's bound and cost ceiling are still open. The harness, isolation and capture design are careful and gated by probes, and most of the decision-layer fixes are textual. The feasibility fix may need more screened candidates or an explicit re-scope to a detection-only study. Re-review after the three blocking findings and majors 4 to 7: M3's interpretation, naming versus judgment, Workflow scoring, and decisions that go beyond what the endpoints measure.

## Findings

- [blocking] (The screen (admission at <=4/8; the fewer-than-ten clause); Decision rules rows 9-11 (T at least 12); Statistics stated once (run counts fixed from the grid); Size (T assumed 12)) I scored each candidate's eight screen runs as binomial at its stated P(R agrees) and admitted it at four of eight or fewer. On that assumption about 9.0 tasks are admitted (SD about 1.8), so P(T>=10) is about 0.39 and P(T>=12) about 0.08, before the unanimity filter removes any. The null rows need T>=12. At T=12 the TOST passes only 0.23 of the time under a true null, and E1 power at the 0.15 bound is 0.41. The rows that would change the configuration are therefore nearly unreachable, and the realistic outcomes are 'positive' or 'inconclusive'. The fewer-than-ten clause registers the trial either way, so its threshold does nothing. 'Run counts are fixed from the grid' names no criterion (target power or budget), which leaves the analyst a free choice. Attempt 1's ceiling result suggests R misses less often than predicted, which would make T smaller still.
  Fix: Register a go/no-go at step 6 with numbers: a minimum T, minimum grid outputs (for example E1 power >=0.8 at the bound and TOST pass >=0.5 under a true null), the rule that picks runs per cell from the grid, and what happens when they are not met (add candidates before the screen, raise runs within the ceiling, or re-scope as detection-only and delete the null rows). State the expected T implied by the predictions in the protocol.
- [blocking] (Decision rules table and the paragraph under it; M1, M2 (Manipulation checks); Anchors specificity condition) The table cannot be applied mechanically. (a) 'F2 significant' and 'F1 significant' carry no sign, so a significant negative F2 (RL beats RS) maps to 'keep the skill', and a significant negative F1 in the null branch maps to 'recommend the RL form'. (b) Rows overlap with no precedence: breadth-not-met and specificity-failed both match; M2 below 0.8 matches both 'not read' and 'inconclusive'; M3 fail matches both 'instrument' and 'inconclusive'. (c) 'The more conservative branch applies' has no ordering of branches. (d) M2 is judged by 'its exact lower 95% bound against 0.8', while the table and I10 use point thresholds. With 70 to 115 RS sessions, a lower bound >=0.8 needs an observed share of about 0.86 to 0.89, so I10 can pass at 8/10 and M2 then fail. (e) 'No increase in over-provisioning' has no test, margin or estimate rule. (f) M1 'pass' is decided on R's absolute naming level, not on the registered R-P test, and the protocol does not say whether the version with or without the patch tasks decides.
  Fix: Sign every condition (F2>0 significant; F1>0 significant). State first-match precedence and order the rows to match. Give a total order for 'conservative'. Define the M2 criterion once and use it in I10. Define specificity as a one-sided bound (for example, the upper 90% bound of RS-R over-provisioning below +0.10). Name the M1 deciding set, and either drop the R-P test or give it a consequence.
- [blocking] (Header (owner items); Decision rules first paragraph; Size cost paragraph; pre-registration step 7) Two inputs are still open. The first is the smallest effect of interest, and whether the null rows use TOST at ±0.15 or an action threshold on an upper bound; the null rows, the power grid and the feasibility check in finding 1 all depend on it. The second is the total cost ceiling, which decides whether the ~930-session plan runs at all.
  Fix: Have the owner fix the bound, its form and the ceiling before the power grid runs, tying the bound to a cost consequence (for example, the tier shift that changes list-price spend by a stated amount). Record both in the manifest.
- [major] (Manipulation checks M3; decision row 8 ('instrument cannot see the skill's content')) M3 is RS-R on two tasks chosen because only the skill's line 56 decides them. If M3 fails, that is evidence the skill's distinctive rules are not transmitted. It is not evidence the instrument is blind, because the capture instrument records every named model and would see any shift. Reading an M3 failure as instrument failure turns a skill null into a non-verdict, which shields the hypothesis from a negative result. The trial has no positive control that works without the skill.
  Fix: Add an instrument positive control that does not use the skill: a directive arm whose always-loaded text names the tier for the planted situation, run on D8 and D13 at the same counts. Let that control carry the 'instrument cannot see' reading, and treat an M3 failure as supporting the null branch.
- [major] (Measurement (Model agreement: an omitted model scores as the session model); The screen; M1) An omitted model scores as Opus, so on haiku- and sonnet-labeled tasks an R session that names no model counts as disagreeing. The screen admits on agreement that includes these omissions, so it favours tasks where R omits. On those tasks E1 can move because the skill makes the orchestrator name any model at all. That is the floor effect the protocol says is not a question. The same selection can push R's naming below 0.9 on the primary set and trip M1. 'Agreement with omitted models excluded' is reported but has no consequence.
  Fix: Screen on agreement among calls that name a model. Alternatively, add a condition to the positive rows: E1 restricted to named calls must have the same sign with an interval excluding 0, or the reading becomes 'naming, not judgment'.
- [major] (Measurement (Scored set: every call must agree; non-literal sites score as non-agreement; Workflow role rule); candidates D14, R6, D15) A workflow task counts as agreeing only if every site agrees, every site has a model literal, and the parser assigns roles correctly. That conjunction lowers agreement in every arm. It is why D14 and R6 are predicted at 0.3 and are the most likely admissions (about 0.94 each), so the primary set will lean on Workflow tasks. The skill's Workflow-scripts and Enforcement sections push the model to write literals, so RS can gain on 'literal written' rather than 'tier chosen'. The role rule ('sites after the fan-out that consume its results') needs data-flow analysis of free-form JavaScript, but it is specified in one sentence plus a per-task override for D15's merger.
  Fix: Register the role-assignment algorithm, with its D15 override and test cases. Make the gate-site model the primary outcome on workflow tasks and the all-sites outcome a sensitivity. Either require E1 to hold with the Workflow tasks dropped as a group, or stratify the primary set by surface with a cap on Workflow tasks.
- [major] (Decision rows 3, 9, 10; Primary (E2 tested only if E1 rejects); Limits (rules of the skill with no task)) 'Recommend the RL form' and 'recommend the rule as it stands' retire skill content the trial never measures: effort placement (line 28), forking (29), missing models (30), workflow-script rules (42) and Enforcement (68). E2, the only effort endpoint, is tested only when E1 rejects, so in the null rows effort goes unmeasured while the recommendation drops the effort rules. 'Keep the skill' rests on transmission of labels that the protocol says it does not validate (Phase 4). RL also adds about 620 always-loaded words to every session, including sessions that never delegate, and no row weighs that cost.
  Fix: Reword rows 3, 9 and 10 to the scope actually measured, along the lines of 'the tier-choice rules transmit as well inline; the rest of the skill is untested'. Test E2 regardless of E1, with its own alpha. Make 'keep' read 'keep, pending Phase 4'. Either weigh RL's always-loaded cost in those rows or state that it is decided outside the trial.
- [major] (Primary E2; Form F1, F2; decision rows 3 and 9) After E1 rejects, E2 and F2 are each tested at 0.05, and 'fixed sequence' is stated only for E2. F1 has no gate at all, yet on its own it drives 'recommend the RL form' in the null branch. The family-wise error of the tests that carry decisions is neither controlled nor stated.
  Fix: Register one scheme. One option is E1 first, then F2 and E2 by Holm at 0.05, with F1 tested only when E1 does not reject, at a stated alpha. The other is to report F1 descriptively and remove it from row 9.
- [major] (Components and arms, RL bullet ('Every primary candidate's governing line is inside RL')) The claim is false as written. D1 and R7 cite lines 18 and 35, and line 35 ('Difficulty is the main axis'), the line that counters their stakes cue, is outside RL. D12 cites line 8, which is also outside. On these tasks F2 measures particular excluded lines and F1 starts at a disadvantage, which tilts the shrink-or-keep decision toward keep.
  Fix: Either add lines 8 and 35 to RL and recount the words, or correct the claim and report F1 and F2 with and without D1, R7 and D12.
- [major] (Probes I7 and I10; pre-registration steps 2 and 5) I7 runs RS capture sessions on D14, D15, D22 and R6, four primary candidates, before the screen and registration. The analyst, who is the skill's author, therefore sees treatment-arm choices on candidate primary tasks before the set is fixed. I10 picks the effort level from RS exposure on 10 sessions without saying whether to move up or down, and 8/10 has an exact lower bound of about 0.44 to 0.49.
  Fix: Run I7 on non-candidate tasks of the same shape, or have the runner output only cost and turns from I7 and seal the transcripts. Fix I10's direction rule and sample size in advance, and use the same criterion as M2.
- [major] (Tasks (label audit paragraph); What attempt 1 could not answer (last row); Limits) Three Sonnet 5.5 instances at one effort level with one handout are one model sampled three times. Their unanimity shows how deterministic that model's reading is; it is not inter-rater agreement. 'Unanimous' is undefined where a labeler gave a second reading (D9, D15, D23). The audit checked model sets only. Effort bands, which E2 uses if the screen switches it to the above-band rate, were never audited.
  Fix: Add at least one labeler of a different model family, or a human. Define unanimity on first readings or on all readings. Either audit the effort bands or keep E2 on 'effort named'.
- [minor] (Arms table (D at 2 runs on primary tasks); Descriptive (D minus RS)) Under Instrument 1 the observer stops the session before the gate's verdict reaches the model, and I4 asserts that RS and D start with byte-identical context. D therefore has no way to differ from RS before the first call, and at 2 runs per cell it could not detect a difference anyway. One asymmetry I4 does not cover: SKILL.md line 68 points to hooks/README.md, which exists in D but not in RS, and Read is granted.
  Fix: Drop D from Instrument 1 (about 38 sessions; the first cut for open question 3), or keep it only as a check on I4. Note the README asymmetry, or ship an identical stub file in RS.
- [minor] (Questions item 4; Instrument 2 'Registered minimum denominator') 'How often does the gate's allow-path reminder change a later delegation' is a causal question. The design has about ten sessions per arm and no within-session counterfactual, so it cannot answer it. The minimum of 10 second delegations 'per arm' is counted in the smoke sessions, which run only arm R, not RS or D. 'The owner's full configuration' overstates a six-tool headless Opus session.
  Fix: Restate Q4 as descriptive. Count the minimum in the gate subset itself, and register what is reported below it. Say 'the plugin's full configuration on this harness'.
- [minor] (Measurement, first bullet (no delegating turn = non-match); candidate D23) D23 ends 'a subagent can do this', which permits doing the work inline. The skill's Scope paragraph says to keep a single dependent chain in one context, so RS can be scored a non-match for following the skill. D23's admission probability is about 0.64.
  Fix: Word every primary prompt as an instruction to delegate. Otherwise, treat no-delegation as missing, with a sensitivity analysis that counts it as a non-match.
- [minor] (Candidates D4 and D5; Findings about the skill text, second bullet) The protocol itself says that line 51 and line 22 both apply to re-verifying a fix on a security subject, and that the skill does not state the tie-break. D4 (the signature validator) and plausibly D5 (the rate limiter) are such tasks, yet they keep the sonnet label in the primary candidates. A screen miss on either can simply be R taking the other defensible reading.
  Fix: Make the union label primary for D4 and D5, or move both to the text-gap block.
- [minor] (What attempt 1 could not answer, row 3) The confound is not removed. R, RL and RS all carry the row's gate claim with no gate present; the claim is held constant across the primary contrast. That is a defensible fix, but the row reads as if the confound were eliminated.
  Fix: Say the gate claim is held constant across R, RL and RS, and that it is true only in D.
- [minor] (Runner rate-limit handling; Failure handling rows 1, 2, 5) Affected cells are re-run 'later', outside the block-randomized order. If rejections or budget-cap endings correlate with arm (RS sessions are longer), the re-runs put one arm's sessions later in time.
  Fix: Re-run the whole block for that task, all arms included, at a fresh randomized position, and record that position.
- [minor] (Harness H2 first and second bullets; probes I1 and I4) The template copies the flag cache and identifiers from a warm-up session, but I1 does not check that no memory, project or session files come along with them. The flag cache can also refresh mid-run. Separately, `blockReadsOutsideWorkingDirectories: true` would block reads of the plugin directory, which sits outside the working directory, while the deny rules and one sensitivity analysis assume those reads are possible.
  Fix: Have I1 assert that the template holds only the listed files. Record the flag-cache hash per session, with the same mismatch rule as the Agent-definition hash. State whether plugin-directory reads are meant to be possible, and confirm it in I4.
- [minor] (Tasks D14, R6, D15 (write and launch a workflow); --tools list; probe I5) The orchestrator has no Write, Edit or Bash, so a workflow must be launched with an inline script. If the Workflow tool needs a file path, the three Workflow candidates cannot run, and no fallback is registered for that case.
  Fix: Add an explicit I5 outcome for this case, saying the Workflow tasks leave the bank or that a script path is pre-seeded.
- [minor] (Measurement (Fork); Orchestrator model and effort (transfer subset)) A fork's tier is not stated; it presumably scores as the session model. In the transfer subset an omitted model scores as Fable rather than Opus, so on opus-labeled tasks an omission flips from agree to disagree between the two models, which confounds the transfer criterion.
  Fix: State that a fork scores as the session model, and report the transfer criterion on named calls only.
- [minor] (Pre-registration step 8; Blinding, leakage and records) Registration is a commit and tag in the author's own repository, and the protocol does not state that the analyst is also the skill's author and owner.
  Fix: Deposit the manifest hash with an external timestamped registry, or in a public issue or gist, and add a one-line conflict-of-interest statement.

## What the result could change

Row 1 (M1 fails): changes nothing; the run is void. Row 2 (positive, F2 significant): settles the open shrink-or-keep question as keep. The configuration stays as it is (rule, skill and gate unchanged), so its only effect is to rule out shrinking, and only if Phase 4 later shows the labels are right. Row 3 (positive, F2 equivalent, F1 significant): the only positive row that changes the configuration. It appends about 620 words to rules/delegation.md and retires or shrinks the skill, leaving the gate alone. It needs F2 equivalence, which passes about 0.23 of the time under a true null at T=12, and it retires untested content. Row 4 (positive, form undecided): no change. Row 5 (driven by one task): no change in table terms; at most an edit to the one skill line that moved, made through the mission review. Row 6 (specificity failed): implies revising the skill because it inflates tiers, though the table names no action; rule and gate unchanged. Row 7 (M2 low, E1 null): points at how the skill is reached (its description or the rule's pointer to it), not its content; no keep-or-drop decision follows. Row 8 (M3 fails, E1 null): changes nothing as written; under finding 4 it should count toward the null. Row 9 (null, equivalent, T>=12, F1 significant): the rule gains the RL text and the skill is retired; gate unchanged. Row 10 (null, equivalent, F1 not significant): the skill is retired and the rule kept; removing its now-dangling pointer to the skill is a rule edit. Gate unchanged. Row 11 (inconclusive): changes nothing. Row 12 (significant negative): implies revising or withdrawing the skill after investigation, but no action is registered. No row changes the gate, because gate data change no branch. No row changes the CLAUDE.md row, because F3 changes no branch. The gate subset, F3, the S and Bm arms, D under Instrument 1 and the transfer subset are all decision-free. Rows 9 and 10 need T>=12, which the screen reaches with probability of about 0.08 under the author's own predictions, and row 3 needs an equivalence result that is rarely reached. The rows that would remove or shrink the skill are therefore the least reachable. The likely outcomes are rows 2, 4, 5 and 11, and none of them changes the configuration.
