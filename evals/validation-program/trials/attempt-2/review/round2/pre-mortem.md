# Pre-mortem

Verdict: The most likely failure is attempt 1's outcome again: 'Inconclusive', produced by the design rather than by the data. All of the following can be computed before registration. At 12 tasks x 6 runs, E1's equivalence branch fires only 12-15% of the time when the true effect is zero (my normal approximation). F2 cannot reach equivalence at all with RL at two runs, and the decision rules give no branch for F2 being neither significant nor equivalent. The ledger lists equivalence power as adopted, but v1's power grid sizes only for detecting a shift. On my approximation, about 16 runs per cell for R and RS (roughly 240 more capture sessions, about $22-31) would reach TOST power 0.8. Separately, M3 rests on one task, D13, and gates both decisive branches, although line 191 scopes it to the null branch. Attempt 1's T17 row (fable in every setup) suggests D13 sits near ceiling in R, where M3 fails about half the time. The per-anchor +/-0.10 band at two runs flags noise on 69-89% of runs with no effect. Second tier: Instrument 2's reminder measure has a near-zero denominator by the pilot's own count (1.00 Agent calls per run), and its forced-denial probe re-measures instruction priority (3 of 3 stopped in attempt 1). The CLI is not pinned across a multi-day plan-usage run. The author's tie-breaks on D4 and D9 can set E1's sign. The per-session cap is calibrated on toy probes, and sessions it ends score as non-matches behind an arm-blind health check. Two further risks did not make the six. The verdict is made on Opus 5.5 while the owner runs Fable 5.1 at max, and no decision rule consults the transfer subset. And I2 picks the session effort for E2's measurability; if that pushes the run to medium, the pilot's 3 of 10 skill invocations for rule plus plugin would fail M2. The probabilities are rough judgments. The M3, anchor and TOST figures come from binomial and normal calculations I ran, under the stated assumptions.

## Inconclusive again: the null and shrink branches could not fire at the registered allocation (p=0.35)

The run completed as planned. Twelve tasks survived the screen; R and RS ran six runs per cell, and RL and the other arms ran two. E1 came out at +0.08 with an exact p of 0.31, and its 90% interval by test inversion ran from about -0.08 to +0.24. The 'no material increment' branch therefore failed its TOST, and the outcome was 'Inconclusive', attempt 1's result in the same words. The allocation nearly forced this. At six runs per cell and agreement near 0.4 to 0.5, the standard error of the task-mean difference over 12 tasks is about 0.08 and the 90% half-width about 0.135 (my normal approximation, with binomial variance and no between-task heterogeneity). The interval fits inside +/-0.15 only when the estimate lands within about +/-0.015 of zero. That happens 12 to 15% of the time when the true effect is exactly zero, and less once the exact test's conservatism widens the interval; v1 line 224 gives a simulated size of 0.02 to 0.03. The form question fared worse. F2 (RS minus RL) compares six runs with two, so its half-width is about 0.19 at p=0.5 and 0.155 at p=0.8. The RL recommendation could not fire at any estimate unless both arms sat at 0.85 or higher. The decision rules (line 214) give no branch for F2 being neither significant nor equivalent, and 'chosen by F1' (line 215) does not say how F1 chooses. The anchors added a misleading sentence. At two runs per cell, an anchor's RS minus R is either 0 or at least +/-0.5. With both arms agreeing on 90% of runs and no true effect, the chance that at least one of six anchors leaves +/-0.10 is 0.89. The write-up duly reported 'the skill moves easy cases'. The ledger (line 101) lists 'derive the bound and add equivalence power' as adopted, but v1's power grid sizes runs only to detect a +0.5 shift. No registered computation checked whether the equivalence branches were reachable.

Earliest signal: The power script, before registration. Adding columns for P(90% interval inside +/-0.15 | true difference 0) at 12 tasks x 6 runs (E1) and at RS 6 / RL 2 (F2), plus the anchors' false-flag rate at two runs per cell, would have shown about 0.12-0.15, 0, and 0.69-0.89.

Prevention: Register equivalence power as a sizing condition: TOST power of at least 0.8 at zero effect. On my approximation that needs about 16 runs per cell for R and RS on 12 tasks (computed 0.80). That is about 240 more capture sessions, roughly $22-31 at v1's $0.09-0.13 capture estimate, and more if task effects vary. The alternative is to replace the TOST with an owner-set action threshold on the upper confidence bound. Give RL the same run count as RS, or delete F2's equivalence branch. Write the missing branch: F2 neither significant nor equivalent means the form is undecided and nothing changes. State how F1 chooses between the RL and rule-only forms. Replace the per-anchor +/-0.10 band with one pooled exact test over the six anchors.

## The gate block measured zero denominators and the user's own instruction (p=0.25)

Instrument 2 ran its 24 sessions and 6 forced denials. The reminder-change rate needs a second orchestrator-level delegation after an allowed first one, and almost no session made one. Each primary task asks for one subagent, one parallel fan-out or one workflow launch, and attempt 1's pilot had already recorded 1.00 Agent calls per delegating run in every setup. The reminder is attached to a call that has already been made, so parallel calls issued in one turn (D2, D8) can never respond to it. Three D sessions on heavy tasks (D11, D13 with a fable worker, D14) timed out at 900 s. Attempt 1 lost 5 of 63 sessions the same way, all on opus-gate, security and workflow tasks (T13, T14, T20), and the primary set over-represents those shapes. The block cost about twice its $25-35 projection, because attempt 1's opus and fable cells had cost $1.4 to $5.8 each. The forced-denial probe reproduced attempt 1's denied-call probe. Told by the user to omit the model, all three orchestrators stopped after the denial (attempt 1: 3 of 3). The three lint cases 'recovered' only by pasting the `/* model-gate:allow */` marker that the denial text supplies. The write-up reported 'reminder changed 0 of 2 measurable second delegations; recovery 0 of 3 and 3 of 3'. The owner read that as 'the reminder does nothing and denials strand sessions', a push toward dropping the gate that neither number supports. No registered rule set a minimum denominator for any Instrument 2 measure, or said that an omission ordered by the user tests instruction priority rather than recovery.

Earliest signal: Already in hand before any new data: PILOT-RESULTS ('mean Agent calls per delegating run 1.00', and the denied-call probe's 3 of 3 stops) and FULL-RESULTS' timeout list. Inside the trial, the one full-execution smoke session per primary task, run before registration, would have counted orchestrator-level delegations per session.

Prevention: Count second orchestrator-level delegations in the smoke sessions and register a minimum, for example 10 per arm. Below it, the reminder measure is reported as unmeasurable rather than as a rate. Alternatively, add two-stage gate tasks whose second delegation depends on the first result (find, then fix what was found). Register that an omission ordered by the user measures instruction priority. Draw the forced denial from a fixture artifact launched as written, and score escape-marker use as a separate outcome, not as recovery. Keep fable- and opus-worker tasks out of Instrument 2, or budget them at attempt 1's per-task costs, with a timeout set from those tasks' observed durations.

## One task gated the verdict, and the screen had already shown it at ceiling (p=0.2)

E1 came out positive and significant, but M3 failed. Both decisive branches require 'M1 to M3 passed' (lines 214-215), so the outcome was 'Inconclusive'. The write-up attached the registered gloss 'instrument cannot see the skill's content', although line 191 scopes M3 to interpreting a null. M3 rested on D13 and its twin alone. In the screen, R chose fable on D13 in 3 of 4 runs. An Opus orchestrator told that an opus xhigh gate had cited a logging line as the signature check escalated on its own reasoning. Every setup, including no text, did the same on attempt 1's T17, the prompt anchor N4 copies. At 0.75, D13 entered the primary set flagged, which capped the expected RS minus R at +0.25, below the +0.3 threshold. With R at 0.75 and RS at 1.0, M3 fails 53% of the time on six runs per arm. Even with real headroom the point threshold is fragile: at R=0.5 and RS=0.9 it fails 26% of the time, and at 0.3 versus 0.8 it fails 16%. A 4-of-4 screen would instead have made D13 an anchor at two runs per cell. There the anchor rule expects +/-0.10 while M3 expects +0.3, and the protocol does not resolve the collision. The gloss was false. The other skill-only rules (D5's re-gate as fix re-verification, D8's lens fan-out) did move. The headline still said the instrument could not see the skill.

Earliest signal: D13's four R runs in the screen, before registration. Earlier still, FULL-RESULTS' T17 row: fable in A, B, C and D.

Prevention: Make M3 conditional on its task screening at R agreement of 0.25 or less, with a pre-written replacement planted task otherwise. Pool M3 over every rule that exists only in the skill text (D5, D8, D13 and their twins). Test the pool with a one-sided exact test or a lower confidence bound above zero, not a point threshold. Say what happens when an M3 task becomes an anchor. Remove M3 from the positive branch's preconditions so it gates only the null reading, as its own definition at line 191 says.

## The CLI updated between the probes and the main run (p=0.2)

The probes ran on 2.1.296 and fixed the effort level (I2), the fork setting (I6) and the observer's behavior (I5). The screen, the label audit and registration took several days. The main run then took several more, paused repeatedly by plan usage-limit errors, which the protocol re-runs. Meanwhile the owner's own sessions kept updating the shared install; between attempt 1 on 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-11 it had already moved from 2.1.295 to 2.1.296. Later chunks ran on a newer build whose Agent tool definition had changed. Attempt 1 had found that same definition varying with configuration on 2.1.295: the effort parameter's presence at a given level and the wording of its 'set this only when asked' description. The per-session record logged the version. The arm-pooled health check counted only errors, observer records, turns and cost, and no registered rule said what a mid-run build change does. The analyst found the change at analysis and pooled the builds under a deviation note written after seeing data. E2, which is scored only where the schema carries `effort`, had different denominators before and after the update. A reviewer could not separate an arm effect from a build effect, and the write-up's validity line ('the Claude Code build recorded', line 263) had to name two builds.

Earliest signal: The Claude Code version field and the recorded Agent tool definition in the first chunk run after the update. Neither appears in the arm-pooled health check.

Prevention: Run every session from a pinned binary by absolute path with the auto-updater disabled in the child; the documented DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER variable is one route, though I did not check it on 2.1.296. Have the runner refuse any session whose version or Agent-tool-definition hash differs from the probes'. Add both to the arm-pooled health check. Register that a change closes the batch at the last complete block, and that the remainder is re-probed and run as a separate stratum or not at all. Re-run I2, I5 and I6 on the day the main run starts.

## The author's tie-breaks decided the sign (p=0.15)

The label audit split on D4 and D9. Two fresh labelers gave opus to D4, citing the security-boundary row at line 22, and one gave opus to D9, citing the reasoning row at line 21. That matches the protocol's own findings (lines 268 and 270): the skill text sets no tie-break for D4 and leaves a first reasoning attempt unlabeled. Both tasks were scored on the author's sonnet label in the primary analysis (line 115). The screen admitted both, because R chose opus there and so missed only the author's reading. In the main run, RS read the skill, met 'security-boundary reads' and 'reasoning-heavy work' in the opus rows, and chose opus as often as R did on D9 and more often on D4. Under the author's label, D4 scored -0.33 and D9 scored 0, while D1, D12 and D22 moved positive. E1 was +0.07 and not significant; the union-label sensitivity analysis gave +0.16 with p 0.03. No rule said which analysis governs when they fall in different branches, so the primary governed by default. The write-up reported 'inconclusive' on a difference produced by two labels the skill text cannot determine. Attempt 1 failed the same way through a scoring reading: its omitted-effort rule 'decides most of the effort result'.

Earliest signal: The label audit before registration, crossed with the screen's tier distribution on the same tasks. When a fresh labeler departs from the author on model and R picks that labeler's model, R 'misses' only under the author's tie-break.

Prevention: Admit to the primary set only tasks whose model label the author and all three labelers share. Screen the other tasks against the union label, and move D4 and D9 to a descriptive text-gap block that feeds the skill-text findings. Resolve the conflict in line 115 between 'a label stands when at least two of three agree' and 'scored on the author's label'. Register that when the primary and union-label analyses land in different decision branches, the more conservative branch applies and the outcome is labeled label-dependent.

## The per-session cap ended RS sessions before their first delegation, out of the health check's sight (p=0.1)

I7 set the per-session cap at three times the capture cost of I5's probe sessions: a one-call toy task, a three-call task and one workflow launch. That came to about $0.35. On the real tasks, RS sessions spent more before delegating. They made a Skill call and loaded the 1,400-word skill, then read validate.js and GUARD.md to write anchors, following the skill's 'rule out setup first ... missing anchors'. On D14 and D15 they also loaded the workflow-authoring reference and wrote a script at high effort. Twenty-one sessions hit the cap before their first delegating turn, sixteen of them in RS or D. The closed exclusion list (line 247) does not include budget exhaustion, so each counted as a session that never delegates: a non-match on every endpoint. The arm-pooled health check showed cost under the chunk ceiling and a small rise in turns, which by design reveals nothing per arm. At analysis, E1 was +0.04 with the cap-ended sessions scored as non-matches and +0.17 with them dropped. The registered sensitivity analysis showed the gap, but no rule said which analysis governs, so a cost setting had pulled the primary toward 'inconclusive'. No probe exercised the cap, so what the `result` message reports when a session hits it was first learned in the main run.

Earliest signal: The smoke run of one case per arm, if it had used the costliest tasks. Otherwise, the first chunk's count of sessions ended by the cap, which the health check does not report.

Prevention: Calibrate the cap on the screen's R sessions and on smoke RS sessions of the costliest tasks (D14, D15, D22), at three times the maximum observed cost. Probe the cap's terminal behavior in I5. Add 'ended by budget before the first delegating turn' to the closed exclusion list, with one re-run at a raised cap. Report the arm-pooled count of cap endings in the health check, with a pre-declared action: any nonzero count raises the cap before the next chunk.
